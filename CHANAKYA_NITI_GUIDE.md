# Chanakya Niti: a detailed guide for study, work and life

Chanakya is one of the most quoted thinkers in India. Parents quote him to children, managers quote him in meetings, and interviewers sometimes ask about him. This guide explains who he was, what the **Chanakya Niti** actually says, how to apply it to studies, interviews, work and leadership, and which parts belong to their own time and should not be followed today.

The verses are given in simple romanised Sanskrit with a plain English meaning. Verse numbers differ between printed editions, so chapters are given as a guide, not an exact reference.

## 1. Who was Chanakya?

**Chanakya** (also called **Kautilya** and **Vishnugupta**) is traditionally described as a Brahmin teacher at **Takshashila** (Taxila, near today's Islamabad), one of the great centres of learning in the ancient world, in the **4th century BCE**.

The story told in Indian tradition:

- He was insulted at the court of **Dhana Nanda**, the ruler of the powerful Nanda kingdom of Magadha (today's Bihar). He vowed to bring the dynasty down.
- He found and trained a young man, **Chandragupta**, and planned the campaign that overthrew the Nandas around **321 BCE**.
- Chandragupta founded the **Maurya Empire**, which under his grandson **Ashoka** covered most of the Indian subcontinent. Chanakya served as his chief adviser.

Much of this comes from later texts and plays, such as the Sanskrit drama **Mudrarakshasa** by Vishakhadatta (written several centuries later), Buddhist and Jain chronicles, and the Puranas. Historians agree that Chandragupta Maurya was real and founded the empire. The details of Chanakya's life are part history, part legend.

### Three works carry his name

| Work | What it is | Style |
|---|---|---|
| **Arthashastra** | A large treatise on statecraft: running a kingdom, economy, law, taxes, spies, diplomacy and war | Prose manual for rulers and officials, 15 books |
| **Chanakya Niti** (also *Chanakya Niti Darpana*) | A collection of short verses of practical wisdom about life, people, money, learning and conduct | Easy-to-remember couplets, usually 17 chapters, roughly 300-350 verses |
| **Chanakya Sutras** | Several hundred one-line maxims on governance and conduct | Very short sayings |

**A note on authorship.** Modern scholars think the **Arthashastra** as we have it was compiled and expanded over time; Patrick Olivelle, who translated it in 2013, dates the text to roughly the 1st century BCE to the 2nd century CE, built on older material. The **Niti** verses also exist in several versions and were collected under Chanakya's name over centuries. So when we say "Chanakya says", we mean "the tradition of wisdom gathered under Chanakya's name". That does not make the advice less useful. It just means we read it as a collection, not as one man's diary.

The Arthashastra itself was lost to the modern world until **1905**, when a manuscript reached **R. Shamasastry** in Mysore. He published it in 1909 and an English translation in 1915.

### What "Niti" means

**Niti** means *the right way of conducting oneself*: practical ethics, policy and good judgement. It sits between two other Sanskrit ideas:

- **Dharma:** what is right, one's duty.
- **Artha:** wealth, livelihood and power, the practical means to live.

Chanakya's work is famous because it treats **artha** seriously. He does not say money and power are bad; he says they must be earned, protected and used wisely, within dharma.

## 2. How to read Chanakya Niti

Four things help:

1. **It is advice for a harsh world.** It was written for kings, ministers, students and householders in a time of wars, famines and court intrigue. Some advice is about survival among people who may cheat you.
2. **It is compressed.** Each verse is a memory aid, not a full argument. Unpack it and ask: *what situation is this for?*
3. **It is often observation, not command.** "A wicked person is worse than a snake" describes people; it does not ask you to treat everyone as a snake.
4. **Some verses reflect the prejudices of their time,** especially about women and caste. Section 12 deals with them honestly. Take the wisdom, leave the prejudice.

## 3. The core idea: know yourself and your situation

This verse is the best one-line summary of Chanakya's thinking:

> **Kah kalah kani mitrani ko deshah kau vyayagamau,**
> **kasyaham ka cha me shaktir iti chintyam muhur muhuh.** (Ch. 4)
>
> *What is the time? Who are my friends? What is the place? What are my income and my expenses? Who am I, and what is my strength? Think about these again and again.*

It is a complete **situation analysis** in six questions:

| Question | Meaning | Today |
|---|---|---|
| **Kah kalah:** what time is it? | Is this the right moment? | Is it placement season, a hiring freeze, a busy quarter? |
| **Kani mitrani:** who are my friends? | Who will support me? | Mentors, seniors, referrals, allies at work |
| **Ko deshah:** what is the place? | Where am I, what are its rules? | This company's culture, this interviewer's style |
| **Kau vyayagamau:** income and expense | What do I gain, what does it cost? | Time, money and energy in versus out |
| **Kasyaham:** who am I? | My role and position | Student, new joiner, manager |
| **Ka cha me shaktih:** what is my strength? | My real capability | Skills I can prove, not claim |

Notice the last word: **muhur muhuh**, "again and again". The situation changes, so the analysis must be repeated. This is very close to modern ideas such as SWOT analysis and situational awareness.

**For Shreya:** before each interview, answer these six questions on paper: Which company and round? Who can tell me about it? What does this company value? How much time do I have and how will I spend it? What role am I applying for? Which two of my projects best prove my strength?

**For a manager:** before a big decision, the same six questions are a quick checklist: timing, stakeholders, organisation context, cost and benefit, your mandate, and your team's real capacity.

## 4. On knowledge and learning

Chanakya valued learning above almost everything else.

> **Kamadhenugunavidya hyakale phaladayini,**
> **pravase matrisadrishi vidya guptam dhanam smritam.** (Ch. 4)
>
> *Knowledge is like the wish-fulfilling cow (Kamadhenu): it gives fruit even in bad seasons. Away from home it cares for you like a mother. Knowledge is called hidden wealth.*

Money can be stolen, lost or taxed. Knowledge goes wherever you go and keeps paying, even in hard times. That is why it is "hidden wealth".

> **Vidya mitram praveseshu bharya mitram griheshu cha,**
> **vyadhitasyaushadham mitram dharmo mitram mritasya cha.** (Ch. 5)
>
> *Knowledge is a friend when travelling, a wife is a friend at home, medicine is a friend to the sick, and dharma is a friend after death.*

Each situation has a right kind of support. On a journey, in a new city or a new job, what you know is what helps you.

> **Pustakastha tu ya vidya parahastagatam dhanam,**
> **karyakale samutpanne na sa vidya na tad dhanam.** (Ch. 16)
>
> *Knowledge that stays in books, and money in someone else's hands: when the time to use them comes, it is neither knowledge nor money.*

This is the single most useful verse for interview preparation. **Knowing where something is written is not knowing it.** If you cannot explain the RAG pipeline, a join or your own project without notes, that knowledge is "in the book". Practise saying things aloud, write code without looking, and teach a topic to someone else. Then the knowledge is yours.

**The five marks of a student** (popularly attributed to Chanakya, though it is a well-known Sanskrit saying found outside the Niti text too):

> **Kaka cheshta, bako dhyanam, shvana nidra tathaiva cha,**
> **alpahari grihatyagi vidyarthi pancha lakshanam.**
>
> *Effort like a crow, focus like a heron, sleep like a dog, eat lightly, and be willing to leave home comforts: these are the five marks of a student.*

- **Crow's effort:** a crow keeps trying until it gets what it wants. Keep at a hard problem.
- **Heron's focus:** a heron stands perfectly still, watching the water, and strikes at the exact moment. Deep, single-minded attention.
- **Dog's sleep:** light sleep, alert to any sound. Today read this as *rest, but do not oversleep*; modern science says students need proper sleep, so do not take it literally.
- **Eat lightly:** a heavy meal makes you sleepy. Do not study on a full stomach.
- **Leave home comforts:** go where learning is, and put up with some discomfort.

Other well-known lines on learning from the Chanakya tradition:

- *Those who have beauty, youth and a great family but no learning do not shine, like the kimshuka flower that has colour but no fragrance.* (Ch. 3) Looks and family name do not replace ability.
- *Take nectar even from poison, gold even from dirt, and good knowledge even from a lowly person.* (Ch. 1) Judge the idea, not the person who says it.

### Applying it to study

1. **Make knowledge "yours":** after reading a topic, close the book and explain it aloud in two minutes. If you cannot, read again.
2. **Heron focus:** study in 45-50 minute blocks with the phone in another room.
3. **Crow effort:** for a LeetCode problem you cannot solve, try for 30 minutes before reading the answer, then solve it again the next day without help.
4. **Learn from anyone:** ask seniors, classmates and even interviewers who rejected you what you could do better.

## 5. On money and wealth

Chanakya is practical about money. Poverty, in his view, is a real misery, and wealth must be earned, saved and spent wisely.

> **Apadarthe dhanam rakshet.** (Ch. 1)
>
> *Save money for times of trouble.*

The full verse goes on to say protect your family even above wealth, and protect yourself above both. In modern terms: **keep an emergency fund**, but never put money above the people you love or your own health.

> **Udyoge nasti daridryam, japato nasti patakam,**
> **maunena kalaho nasti, nasti jagarato bhayam.** (Ch. 3)
>
> *There is no poverty for the hard-working, no sin for one who prays, no quarrel for one who stays silent, no fear for one who stays alert.*

Four cause-and-effect rules in one verse. Effort removes poverty; silence avoids quarrels; alertness removes fear.

Other teachings on money:

- **Spend with care.** The Niti repeatedly warns against careless spending and waste: wealth that is not looked after disappears, however much is earned.
- **Money in other people's hands is not yours** (see the verse in section 4). Lending carelessly, or depending on money you are "owed", leaves you helpless when you need it.
- **Honest wealth lasts.** *Anyayoparjitam dravyam dasha varshani tishthati* (Ch. 15): wealth gained unjustly stays for ten years, and in the eleventh it is lost, root and all. Quick gains by unfair means are a bad trade.
- **Wealth is a means.** It should support family, learning, charity and security, not become a show.

**For a student or new joiner:** the first salary is the time to build habits. Save first and spend what remains; build three to six months of expenses as an emergency fund; learn what you sign (loans, bonds, insurance). This is general advice, not personal financial advice.

## 6. On friends, enemies and trust

This is the part of Chanakya people remember most, because it is unsentimental.

### Test people in the right situation

> **Janiyat preshane bhrityan bandhavan vyasanagame,**
> **mitram chapattikaleshu bharyam cha vibhavakshaye.** (Ch. 1)
>
> *Know your servants when you give them a task, relatives when calamity comes, a friend in adversity, and a spouse when wealth is lost.*

Everyone is pleasant when things go well. **Character shows under pressure.** At work: you learn who your real team is in a crisis, a failed launch or a bad review, not at a celebration.

### Beware the false friend

> **Parokshe karyahantaram pratyakshe priyavadinam,**
> **varjayet tadrisham mitram vishakumbham payomukham.** (Ch. 2)
>
> *A friend who praises you to your face but ruins your work behind your back: avoid him. He is a pot of poison with milk on top.*

A sweet tongue is not proof of goodwill. Watch what people **do**, especially when you are not in the room.

> **Sarpah krurah khalah krurah sarpat krurataram khalah,**
> **mantraushadhivashah sarpah khalah kena nivaryate.** (Ch. 3)
>
> *A snake is cruel and a wicked person is cruel, but the wicked person is crueller. A snake can be controlled by charms and medicine; what can control a wicked person?*

### Keep your plans to yourself

> **Manasa chintitam karyam vachasa na prakashayet,**
> **mantravad rakshayed gudham karye chapi niyojayet.** (Ch. 2)
>
> *Do not reveal by speech what you have planned in your mind. Guard it like a secret mantra, and put it into action.*

Many plans fail because they are announced too early: others copy, block or discourage them. **Do the work first, then talk.**

Related advice: do not tell your weaknesses, your private troubles, your losses or insults you have suffered to people who may use them against you.

### Do not be too straight

> **Natyantam saralair bhavyam gatva pashya vanasthalim,**
> **chhidyante saralas tatra kubjas tishthanti padapah.** (Ch. 7)
>
> *Do not be too straightforward. Go and look at a forest: the straight trees are cut down, the crooked ones are left standing.*

This is often misread as "be crooked". It means: **do not be naive.** Being honest is good; being so simple that anyone can take advantage of you is not. Be honest, but read the situation, protect yourself, and do not overshare.

### Friends and enemies change

Chanakya's statecraft (the **mandala** theory in the Arthashastra) is the source of the famous idea that **the enemy of my enemy is my friend**. Neighbouring kingdoms are natural rivals; the kingdom beyond them is a natural ally. In daily life the lesson is gentler: relationships are shaped by interests, so understand what each person needs.

### Applying it at work

- **Judge by actions over time,** not by flattery or first impressions.
- **Say less about plans that are not ready.** Share them when they are, with the people who need to know.
- **Keep your circle honest:** people who tell you hard truths are worth more than people who only praise.
- **Do not become cynical.** Chanakya warns about bad people so that you can trust good ones with open eyes, not so that you trust nobody.

## 7. On speech, anger and self-control

- **Speak sweetly, and at the right time.** The Niti says the whole world can be won by kind words, so why be poor in speech?
- **Silence prevents quarrels** (see "maunena kalaho nasti" in section 5).
- **Anger is like Yama, the god of death; craving is like the Vaitarani, the river of torment in the underworld; knowledge is like the wish-fulfilling cow; contentment is like Nandana, the garden of the gods.** (*Krodho vaivasvato raja, trishna vaitarani nadi*, Ch. 8.)
- **Moderation in everything:**

> **Atirupena vai Sita atigarvena Ravanah,**
> **atidanad Balir baddho, ati sarvatra varjayet.** (Ch. 3)
>
> *Because of too much beauty Sita was carried away; because of too much pride Ravana was destroyed; because of too much giving King Bali was bound. Avoid excess in everything.*

Even good things, like generosity and confidence, become harmful in excess. In an interview, too much modesty hides your work; too much confidence sounds like arrogance. Aim for the middle.

**For interviews:** when an interviewer pushes back, Chanakya's advice is the same as modern advice: stay calm, do not argue, think before you speak, and answer clearly. "That's a good point, let me reconsider" is pure niti.

## 8. On time, effort and action

> **Gate shoko na kartavyo bhavishyam naiva chintayet,**
> **vartamanena kalena vartayanti vichakshanah.** (Ch. 13)
>
> *Do not grieve over the past, do not worry about the future. The wise act in the present.*

This is the best verse for placement season. A rejection yesterday cannot be changed; tomorrow's interview cannot be controlled. **What you do today** is the only thing in your hands.

Other teachings on action:

- **Before you start any work, ask three questions: Why am I doing it? What will the result be? Will I succeed?** Only when you have good answers, go ahead. (A popular Chanakya teaching in modern collections.)
- **Once you start, do not give up because of fear of failure.** People who work sincerely are the happiest.
- **Learn one thing from a lion:** whatever task you take on, big or small, do it with full effort. The Niti lists lessons from animals: from the lion, full effort; from the heron, controlled focus; from the rooster, waking early and sharing; from the crow, alertness; from the dog, loyalty and contentment.
- **Time is the great consumer.** Time eats all beings; it is awake when everyone sleeps. You cannot buy back a wasted day.

### A practical daily routine in Chanakya's spirit

| When | What | Chanakya idea |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Review today's three most important tasks | "Kah kalah": know the moment |
| Study or work blocks | 45-50 minutes of single focus | Heron's focus |
| After each block | Write or say what you learned without notes | Knowledge must not stay in books |
| Evening | Note one mistake and one lesson; then let the day go | Do not grieve over the past |
| Weekly | Review money, health and relationships | Income and expense; know your friends |

## 9. On character and company

> **Ekenapi suvrikshena pushpitena sugandhina,**
> **vasitam tad vanam sarvam suputrena kulam yatha.** (Ch. 3)
>
> *A single good tree, in flower and fragrant, perfumes the whole forest, just as one good child brings honour to the whole family.*

One person of good character lifts everyone around them. In a team, one generous, competent person changes the whole culture.

- **Choose company carefully.** The Niti says good company makes even an ordinary person respected, and bad company drags a good one down.
- **Character is shown, not claimed.** Gold is tested by rubbing, cutting, heating and beating; a person is tested by their sacrifice, conduct, qualities and actions. (A well-known Niti verse.)
- **Birth does not make a person great; deeds do.** Several verses praise learning and conduct over family name.

### Where to live and work

> **Yasmin deshe na sammano na vrittir na cha bandhavah,**
> **na cha vidyagamah kashchit tam desham parivarjayet.** (Ch. 1)
>
> *Do not live in a place where you get no respect, no livelihood, no friends or relatives, and no chance to learn.*

A useful test for a job offer: **respect, pay, people and learning.** If a role offers none of these, Chanakya would tell you to leave. If it offers learning and respect, a slightly lower salary early in your career may be worth it.

Another verse lists what a place needs: a wealthy trader, a learned person, a just ruler, a river and a doctor. In other words: an economy, education, rule of law, water and healthcare. It is a remarkably modern checklist for a liveable city.

## 10. On family and raising children

> **Lalayet pancha varshani dasha varshani tadayet,**
> **prapte tu shodashe varshe putram mitravad acharet.** (Ch. 3)
>
> *Pamper a child for five years, discipline them for the next ten, and once they reach sixteen, treat them as a friend.*

The idea of stages is still useful: **warmth in early childhood, structure in the school years, and friendship and respect with a young adult.** The word *tadayet* literally includes physical punishment, which we reject today; read "discipline" as clear rules and consequences.

Other family teachings:

- A home is happy when children are obedient, the spouse is supportive, money is honestly earned and enough, and friends are true.
- Parents who do not educate their children are, in the Niti's strong words, their enemies, because an uneducated child is out of place among the learned, "like a heron among swans".
- Do not over-pamper: many faults come from too much indulgence, many virtues from discipline.

## 11. Chanakya on leadership and governance

The **Arthashastra** is a full manual of government. A few of its ideas are directly useful to anyone who leads a team.

### The king serves the people

> **Prajasukhe sukham rajnah prajanam cha hite hitam,**
> **natmapriyam hitam rajnah prajanam tu priyam hitam.** (Arthashastra, Book 1)
>
> *In the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare, his welfare. What pleases himself is not good for the king; what pleases his subjects is.*

This is servant leadership written over two thousand years ago. **A leader's success is the team's success.**

### The leader must be disciplined first

The Arthashastra gives the king a demanding daily timetable, dividing day and night into slots for hearing reports, receiving citizens, reviewing finances, meeting ministers, planning and only a little sleep. The point: **a leader who does not manage their own time cannot manage a kingdom.** Discipline (vinaya) and self-control come before power.

### The seven pillars of a state (saptanga)

| Pillar | Meaning | In an organisation |
|---|---|---|
| **Swami** | The ruler | Leadership |
| **Amatya** | Ministers and officials | Managers and key people |
| **Janapada** | Territory and people | Customers, market and users |
| **Durga** | Fortified places | Resilience: systems, backups, security |
| **Kosha** | Treasury | Budget and cash |
| **Danda** | Army and the power to enforce | Execution capacity, rules that are enforced |
| **Mitra** | Allies | Partners and stakeholders |

A weakness in any pillar weakens the whole. It is a useful way to review any team or business.

### Four ways to deal with others (chaturopaya)

| Method | Meaning | Modern equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **Sama** | Conciliation, persuasion | Explain, align, build agreement |
| **Dana** | Gifts, giving something | Negotiate, offer incentives, trade-offs |
| **Bheda** | Division, splitting opponents | Use competing interests (handle with ethics) |
| **Danda** | Force, punishment | Escalation, enforcement, formal consequences |

The order matters: **start with persuasion and use force last.** At work, try explanation and negotiation before escalation.

### Information and checks

The Arthashastra relies heavily on information: reports, inspections and a network of informants, and it assumes officials may be tempted. A famous line compares this to fish in water: just as you cannot tell when a fish drinks water, you cannot easily tell when an official takes money. Its answer is **checks and audits**: rotate roles, verify accounts, and reward honesty. Modern equivalents are clear metrics, audits, separation of duties and transparency.

### Choosing people

Ministers should be tested before appointment, for loyalty, honesty, courage and resistance to temptation, and chosen for ability rather than only family or friendship. In hiring terms: **test for the real skill and the real character, and do not hire someone only because you like them.**

### Applying it as a manager or director

- **Make the team's success your success**, and say so publicly.
- **Know your seven pillars:** people, customers, resilience, budget, execution and partners. Which one is weakest this quarter?
- **Persuade first, escalate last.**
- **Trust, but verify:** clear goals, measurable outcomes and regular reviews protect good people as well as catching problems.
- **Keep your counsel:** discuss sensitive plans with a small trusted group before announcing them.

## 12. What not to follow today

An honest reading must say clearly: some verses in the Chanakya Niti are **not acceptable** by today's values.

- **Women.** Several verses describe women as untrustworthy, deceitful or of lesser judgement, and list "faults" of women in general. These reflect the prejudices of their era. They are wrong, and they should not be quoted as wisdom.
- **Caste and birth.** Some verses assume a fixed social order by birth. Modern India's Constitution rejects discrimination by caste, and so should we. (Note that other verses in the same text praise learning and conduct above birth.)
- **Physical punishment of children.** As noted in section 10, read "discipline" as structure, not beating.
- **Means justify ends.** The Arthashastra allows deception, spies, poison and assassination for reasons of state. Calling someone "Chanakyan" can mean "cunning". In a workplace, manipulation and deception destroy trust, careers and companies. Use Chanakya's **clear thinking** and **planning**, not his **spycraft**.

A simple rule: **keep the advice that makes you wiser, more disciplined and more careful; drop anything that makes you look down on a group of people or harm someone.**

## 13. Chanakya for Shreya: placement season edition

| Situation | Chanakya idea | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Preparing for a company | Kah kalah, ko deshah: know the time and place | Research the company, the round and the role |
| "Tell me about yourself" | Speak sweetly and at the right time; avoid excess | 60-90 seconds, confident but not boastful |
| Technical questions | Knowledge in books is not knowledge | Practise explaining aloud without notes |
| A question you cannot answer | Silence prevents quarrels; honesty with judgement | "I haven't worked on that directly, but my approach would be..." |
| Interviewer disagrees | Control anger; think before speaking | "That's a good point, let me reconsider." |
| A rejection | Do not grieve over the past | Write one lesson, then prepare for the next |
| Comparing offers | Respect, livelihood, people, learning | Score each offer on these four |
| Group discussion | One good tree perfumes the forest | Add a good point, bring others in, summarise fairly |
| Studying with friends | Choose company carefully | Study with people who push you to do better |

### If an interviewer asks "Who inspires you?" or about Indian thinkers

You can mention Chanakya, briefly and honestly:

"I find Chanakya's practical side useful. One verse asks you to keep asking yourself: what is the time, who are my friends, where am I, what are my costs, who am I, and what is my strength. I use that before every big decision, like choosing which projects to focus on for placements."

Keep it short and connect it to a real action. Do not quote verses you cannot explain.

## 14. Chanakya for a manager and leader

| Situation | Chanakya idea | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Planning a quarter | Six-question situation analysis | Timing, allies, context, cost, mandate, team capacity |
| A new initiative | Do not reveal plans too early | Socialise with key stakeholders first, then announce |
| Hiring | Test ministers before appointing | Structured interviews; check skill and character |
| Conflict between teams | Sama before danda | Align on goals, negotiate, escalate only if needed |
| A failing project | Test people in adversity | Notice who steps up; recognise them |
| Your own promotion case | Knowledge must be usable; one good tree | Show visible impact that lifts the whole org |
| Team culture | The king's happiness is the people's happiness | Credit the team; protect their time and growth |
| Risk | Save for bad times; durga (fortress) | Buffers in plans, budgets and systems |

## 15. Glossary

- **Artha:** wealth, livelihood, power and the practical means of life. One of the four aims of life.
- **Dharma:** right conduct, duty, what holds society together.
- **Kama:** desire and pleasure. **Moksha:** liberation. Together with artha and dharma, the four aims of life (purusharthas).
- **Niti:** right conduct, practical wisdom, policy.
- **Arthashastra:** "the science of artha", Kautilya's treatise on statecraft.
- **Saptanga:** the seven limbs or pillars of the state.
- **Upaya:** a means or method; **chaturopaya** are the four methods (sama, dana, bheda, danda).
- **Mandala:** the "circle of states" theory of neighbours, enemies and allies.
- **Vinaya:** discipline and training, especially of a ruler.
- **Takshashila (Taxila):** the ancient centre of learning where Chanakya is said to have taught.
- **Maurya Empire:** the empire founded by Chandragupta Maurya around 321 BCE.

- **Chanakya Niti** in Hindi or English with commentary: many inexpensive editions exist; choose one that gives the Sanskrit verse alongside the meaning.
- **Corporate Chanakya** by **Radhakrishnan Pillai**: a popular book applying Arthashastra ideas to business and management.

## Key takeaways

- Chanakya's tradition is **practical wisdom**: think clearly, plan carefully, work hard, protect yourself and serve others.
- The **six questions** (time, friends, place, cost, who am I, my strength) are a timeless situation analysis. Ask them again and again.
- **Knowledge you cannot use without the book is not yours.** Practise until you can explain without notes.
- **Judge people by actions under pressure**, keep plans quiet until they are ready, and do not be naive.
- **Avoid excess, act in the present,** and do every task with full effort.
- **A leader's happiness lies in the people's happiness.** Persuade first, escalate last; trust, but verify.
- **Keep the wisdom, leave the prejudice:** reject the verses on women and caste, and do not copy the spycraft.
